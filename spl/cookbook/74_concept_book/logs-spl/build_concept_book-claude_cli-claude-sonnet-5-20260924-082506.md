## Chrononutrition

Your body doesn't respond to food the same way at 8 a.m. as it does at 8 p.m. The idea behind chrononutrition is that *when* you eat matters almost as much as *what* you eat, because your body runs on an internal circadian clock — a roughly 24-hour cycle that adjusts hormone levels, digestion, and metabolism depending on the time of day.

One of the clearest examples is insulin sensitivity. Insulin is the hormone that helps cells pull glucose (sugar) out of the blood to use for energy. Research shows insulin sensitivity is higher in the morning and early afternoon, meaning a given meal triggers a smaller, more efficient insulin response than the *same* meal eaten late at night. By evening, the body becomes more insulin-resistant — it needs more insulin to handle the same amount of glucose, and blood sugar tends to stay elevated longer.

This is why chrononutrition often recommends front-loading calories earlier in the day — a bigger breakfast and lunch, a lighter dinner — and finishing eating a few hours before bed. Eating close to sleep forces the body to manage digestion and blood sugar regulation while it's biologically primed to wind down, not process food.

**Simple rule:** align bigger meals with your body's more insulin-sensitive hours (morning through mid-afternoon), and give yourself a buffer of 2–3 hours between your last meal and bedtime.
