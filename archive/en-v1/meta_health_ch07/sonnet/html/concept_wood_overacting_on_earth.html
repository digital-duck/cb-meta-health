Wood Overacting On Earth

Stress before an exam. Your stomach tightens, you skip lunch, or you feel a sudden urge to run to the bathroom. TCM has a name for this pattern: the Liver (Wood) "overacting" on the Spleen (Earth), or 肝木克脾土.

Recall the Five Element cycle: Wood normally controls Earth in a gentle, balancing way — like tree roots holding soil in place, keeping it from washing away unchecked. That's healthy regulation. But when Liver qi gets stuck — from frustration, anxiety, or bottled-up stress — that control turns into an attack. The system that should stabilize digestion instead disrupts it.

Here's the structural logic: the Liver's job is to keep qi moving smoothly throughout the body. When qi stagnates, it doesn't just sit still — it presses outward on nearby systems, and the Spleen/Stomach (responsible for digestion) is the most common target. This explains why stress so reliably produces gut symptoms: bloating, a knotted feeling under the ribs, poor appetite, or alternating constipation and loose stools.

The simple rule: excess in the controlling element disrupts function in the controlled element. Wood-over-Earth is diagnosed when digestive symptoms co-occur with signs of Liver qi stagnation — irritability, sighing, a tight sensation in the chest or sides — rather than appearing alone.
