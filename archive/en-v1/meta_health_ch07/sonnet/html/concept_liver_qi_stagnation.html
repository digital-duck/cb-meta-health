Liver Qi Stagnation

You already know the feeling: something bothers you, you don't say it, and by the end of the day your chest feels tight and you keep sighing without meaning to. In Chinese medicine this pattern has a name — Liver qi stagnation (肝气郁结) — and it's worth studying as a structure, not just a mood.

The Liver's job in this system is to keep qi (气, the body's functional energy) moving smoothly in every direction, the way a river needs a clear channel. When emotion — especially frustration, resentment, or things left unsaid — isn't expressed, the flow gets blocked at a specific site. Think of it like a traffic jam: the qi is not gone, it's just backed up, and pressure builds behind the blockage.

That backup shows up in predictable places along the Liver's pathway: the sides of the ribs (flank tightness), the throat (a "plum-pit" lump sensation called 梅核气, méi hé qì), and mood itself (irritability, sighing as the body's attempt to force qi through). Note the pattern: physical tension and emotional tension are treated as one system, not two separate problems — this is the core rule to remember. Stagnation always seeks an outlet, whether that's a sigh, a symptom, or an outburst.

Simple rule: unexpressed emotion → blocked qi flow → localized tension where the pathway narrows.
