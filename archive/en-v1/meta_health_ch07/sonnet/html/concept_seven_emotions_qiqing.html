Seven Emotions Qiqing

You already know that emotions aren't just "in your head" — a scary movie makes your heart pound, and nervousness before a test can upset your stomach. Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) built an entire framework around this connection, organizing human emotional life into seven categories: joy (喜), anger (怒), worry (忧), pensiveness (思), grief (悲), fear (恐), and fright (惊). TCM calls these the seven emotions, or qiqing.

Here's the key structural idea: TCM doesn't treat emotions as separate from physical health. Each of the seven emotions is paired with a specific internal organ system, and when an emotion becomes too intense or lasts too long, it's believed to injure that organ's function. For example, excessive anger is linked to the Liver, prolonged worry to the Lungs, and pensiveness (overthinking) to the Spleen. Notice the pattern: it's not the emotion itself that's the problem — brief anger or occasional worry are normal parts of life — it's the intensity or duration that crosses into causing illness.

This gives us a simple rule: moderate emotion is healthy; extreme or chronic emotion is a disease cause. TCM treats emotional balance as one input into physical health, alongside diet and environment — not a separate category from it.
