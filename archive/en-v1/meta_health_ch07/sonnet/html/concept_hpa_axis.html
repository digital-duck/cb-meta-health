Hpa Axis

You already know the basic idea: something stresses you out, and your body reacts. The HPA axis is the actual chain of command behind that reaction — a relay system connecting three organs: the hypothalamus, the pituitary gland, and the adrenal glands.

Here's how the relay works, step by step:

  • The hypothalamus (in your brain) detects a stressor and releases a signaling hormone called CRH (corticotropin-releasing hormone).
  • CRH travels a short distance to the pituitary gland, telling it to release ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone) into the bloodstream.
  • ACTH travels through blood to the adrenal glands (sitting on top of your kidneys), triggering them to release cortisol — the main stress hormone.

    • Notice the pattern: each organ doesn't act alone — it sends a chemical messenger to activate the next one. This is a signaling cascade, and it's common throughout the body wherever a small signal needs to be amplified into a larger, body-wide response.

    There's also a feedback loop worth knowing: once cortisol reaches a high enough level in the blood, it signals back to the hypothalamus and pituitary to stop releasing CRH and ACTH. This is called negative feedback — the same principle a thermostat uses to keep a room from overheating.
