Seasonal Food And Movement

Think about how your instinct already shifts with the seasons — a cold glass of water sounds amazing on a July afternoon, but in January you'd rather wrap your hands around a warm mug. Traditional health practices took that instinct and built it into a system: what you eat and how you move should change with the season, because your body's needs actually change too.

Here's the structure behind it. In summer, the body's energy is naturally pushed outward and upward — think more blood flow near the skin surface (helping you sweat and cool down), higher activity, longer daylight. The matching practice: lighter, cooling foods (fruits, vegetables, more water) and movement scheduled in the cooler early morning, when your body isn't already working hard to manage heat. In winter, energy pulls inward and downward — the body prioritizes keeping its core warm, digestion slows slightly, daylight is short. The matching practice: warming, nourishing foods (cooked grains, soups, warming spices) and gentler, shorter movement sessions, since pushing hard in cold, low-light conditions adds stress rather than benefit.

There's also an organ-specific layer: each season is traditionally linked to supporting a particular organ system, so movement and diet in that season are chosen partly to gently "exercise" that system rather than strain it.

The simple rule: match your food's temperature and your workout's intensity to the season's energy — cool and light when the world is expanding outward, warm and gentle when it's contracting inward.
