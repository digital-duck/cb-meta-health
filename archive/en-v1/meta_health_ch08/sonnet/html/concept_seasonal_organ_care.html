Seasonal Organ Care

By now you know the five-phase cycle: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water, each paired with an organ. Seasonal organ care applies that cycle directly to the calendar. In Chinese medicine, five seasons map onto the five organs — spring nourishes the Liver, summer nourishes the Heart, late summer nourishes the Spleen, autumn nourishes the Lung, and winter nourishes the Kidney. This isn't a coincidence layered on top of the theory — it is the theory, applied in time.

Why does this mapping make sense structurally? Each season carries the qi (energy pattern) of its phase. Spring's rising, expansive energy resembles Wood's tendency to grow and spread, so it's said to stress or activate the Liver, the organ governing smooth flow. Summer's peak heat mirrors Fire and the Heart. Late summer's transitional, "ripening" quality mirrors Earth and the Spleen, which governs transformation. Autumn's contracting dryness mirrors Metal and the Lung. Winter's stillness mirrors Water and the Kidney, the body's reserve of stored essence.

The simple rule: each season's climate places extra demand on its matched organ, so care shifts to match. In spring, avoid frustration and encourage gentle movement (support Liver's smooth flow). In summer, avoid overexertion in heat (protect Heart). In late summer, eat warm, easily digested food (support Spleen). In autumn, keep lungs moist and avoid dry air (protect Lung). In winter, rest and conserve energy (protect Kidney).
