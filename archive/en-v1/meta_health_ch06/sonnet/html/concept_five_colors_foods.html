Five Colors Foods

Think back to the meal-planning idea: pick foods across colors and you're likely covering more nutrients without needing a spreadsheet. TCM formalizes that intuition into a system called wu se (五色, "five colors"), where each color maps to one of the five organ systems:

$$\text{green} \to \text{Liver}, \quad \text{red} \to \text{Heart}, \quad \text{yellow} \to \text{Spleen}, \quad \text{white} \to \text{Lung}, \quad \text{black} \to \text{Kidney}$$

This isn't decoration — it's the same five-phase (wu xing, 五行) framework you've seen organize seasons, tastes, and emotions, now applied to what's on your plate. Each color-organ pair is a category, not a strict rule: a food is assigned based on its dominant visual and functional profile, similar to how you might classify a shape by its most prominent feature even if it has minor irregularities.

The useful pattern: if you can identify all five colors across a day's meals, you've structurally distributed support across all five organ systems — no single system is neglected. This is a low-precision heuristic (a food's color doesn't perfectly predict its biochemical effect), but it functions like a checksum: a quick structural check for balance, not a nutritional calculation.

Practice problem: Yesterday you ate: spinach (green), rice (white/yellow?), and black sesame seeds. List the two color-organ pairs you clearly hit, identify one color you're missing, and name one food that would fill that gap.
