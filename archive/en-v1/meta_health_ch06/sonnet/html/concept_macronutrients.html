Macronutrients

You already know that food gives your body energy and materials to grow and repair — but "food" isn't one thing. It breaks down into three macronutrients: protein, carbohydrate, and fat. Each plays a distinct role, and your body needs all three in different amounts.

Think of macronutrients as raw materials for two jobs: building and fueling. Protein is mostly a builder — it supplies amino acids your body links into muscle, enzymes, and hormones. Carbohydrate and fat are mostly fuel — they get broken down to release energy your cells can use. Fat is also stored as a long-term energy reserve, and some fats are structural, forming parts of every cell membrane.

Here's a useful pattern for measuring this: each macronutrient releases a different amount of energy per gram when your body metabolizes it, measured in kilocalories (kcal):

$$ 1\text{ g protein} \approx 4\text{ kcal}, \quad 1\text{ g carbohydrate} \approx 4\text{ kcal}, \quad 1\text{ g fat} \approx 9\text{ kcal} $$

Notice fat supplies more than double the energy per gram. That's why a small amount of fat in a meal can contribute a large share of its total calories — it's also why fat is such an efficient way for the body to store energy for later.

Practice problem: A snack contains 10 g protein, 20 g carbohydrate, and 5 g fat. Using the values above, calculate the total energy in kcal, and find what percentage of that energy comes from fat.
