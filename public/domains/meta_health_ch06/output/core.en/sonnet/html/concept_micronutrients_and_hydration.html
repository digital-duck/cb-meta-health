Micronutrients And Hydration

You already know food gives you energy — but not all nutrients are about calories. Micronutrients (vitamins and minerals) and water don't get "burned" for fuel the way carbohydrates or fats do. Instead, they act as cofactors: small helper molecules that let your body's chemical machinery actually run.

Think of your metabolism as a factory full of enzymes — proteins that speed up chemical reactions. Many enzymes are useless without a specific cofactor attached, like a lock that only turns with the right key. Iron sits at the center of hemoglobin, letting your blood carry oxygen. Magnesium is required for over 300 enzyme reactions, including ones that build ATP, your cells' energy currency. B vitamins (like B1, B2, B3) act as coenzymes in the pathways that convert glucose into usable energy. Without them, the "engine" of cellular respiration stalls even if you have plenty of fuel.

Water plays a different but equally essential role: it's the solvent where these reactions happen, and it carries dissolved ions (like $\text{Na}^+$, $\text{K}^+$, $\text{Cl}^-$) that generate the electrical signals nerves and muscles use to communicate.

A useful pattern: macronutrients supply the fuel and building material; micronutrients and water make the reactions possible. You need macronutrients in grams, but micronutrients only in milligrams or micrograms — small doses, big leverage.
