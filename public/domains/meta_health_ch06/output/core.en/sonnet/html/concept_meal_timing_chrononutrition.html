Meal Timing Chrononutrition

You already know your body runs on a rough 24-hour cycle — you get sleepy at night and alert in the morning. That internal schedule is called the circadian clock, and it doesn't just control sleep. It also controls how well your digestive system works at different times of day.

Here's the structure behind that intuition: your circadian clock adjusts hormone levels, enzyme activity, and blood flow to your gut on a schedule. In the morning and early afternoon, your body is primed to break down food efficiently — insulin sensitivity is higher, digestive enzymes are more active, and gut motility (how fast food moves through your intestines) is closer to normal. But late at night, your body is shifting toward rest-and-repair mode. Insulin sensitivity drops, motility slows, and digestion becomes less efficient.

So eating the same meal at 7 PM versus 11 PM isn't neutral — it's a mismatch between food intake and your body's internal readiness. Late-night eating forces your digestive organs to "work overtime" against their natural rhythm, similar to how running a factory shift when the workers are already exhausted produces worse output with more strain.

Simple rule: the same meal, eaten earlier, is processed with less physiological cost than when eaten late at night — timing matters, not just content.
