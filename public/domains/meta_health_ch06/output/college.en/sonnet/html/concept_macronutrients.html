Macronutrients

Macronutrients are the nutrients the body needs in large amounts (grams per day, not milligrams): protein, carbohydrate, and fat. They do two jobs. They supply energy, and they supply raw material for building and maintaining tissue. Water and alcohol are sometimes discussed alongside them, but the three above are the core of nutrition planning. Vitamins and minerals, called micronutrients, supply no energy.

Energy values

Food energy is measured in kilocalories (kcal). The standard Atwater values are:

| Macronutrient | Energy (kcal per gram) | Main roles | |---|---|---| | Carbohydrate | 4 | Preferred fuel for brain and working muscle; stored as glycogen | | Protein | 4 | Building and repairing muscle, enzymes, hormones, immune proteins | | Fat | 9 | Concentrated energy store; cell membranes; absorption of vitamins A, D, E and K |

Fat carries more than twice the energy per gram of the other two. This is why a small amount of oil adds many calories to a meal.

Worked example

A serving of granola is labeled: 30 g carbohydrate, 8 g protein, 12 g fat. Find its energy and the share that comes from fat.

$$ E = 30(4) + 8(4) + 12(9) = 120 + 32 + 108 = 260 \text{ kcal} $$

The fat share is:

$$ \frac{108}{260} \approx 0.42 $$

So about 42% of the calories come from fat, even though fat is only 24% of the food's mass in grams ($12/50$). Comparing calories and grams gives different pictures, and you need to know which one a recommendation uses.

Problem-solving application

A common reference range for adults is 45–65% of energy from carbohydrate, 10–35% from protein, and 20–35% from fat. Suppose a person eats 2,200 kcal per day and wants 20% of that from protein.

  • Protein energy: $0.20 \times 2200 = 440$ kcal.
  • Convert to grams: $\frac{440}{4} = 110$ g of protein.

    • Check this against body size. For a 70 kg person, 110 g is about 1.6 g per kg of body mass. That is within the range often suggested for people doing regular strength training (roughly 1.2–2.0 g/kg). It is well above the 0.8 g/kg minimum recommended for sedentary adults. The percentage target and the per-kilogram target can disagree, so check both.

    The same method works in reverse. If a meal plan gives 250 g carbohydrate, 90 g protein, and 70 g fat, the total is $1000 + 360 + 630 = 1990$ kcal. Fat supplies $\frac{630}{1990} \approx 32\%$, inside the reference range.

    Key points

  • Energy comes from carbohydrate and protein at 4 kcal/g and from fat at 9 kcal/g.
  • Protein is the main source of amino acids for building tissue. It is used for energy mainly when other fuel is short.
  • Convert between grams and percentages using the energy values, and check totals against a person's body size and activity.
