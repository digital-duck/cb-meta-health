Eating for Your Constitution (体质)

Definition. In Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), a constitution (体质, tǐzhì) is a person's relatively stable pattern of body tendencies, such as how warm or cold they run, how easily they tire, and how they handle fluids and digestion. A widely used modern classification divides people into nine types: balanced, qi-deficient, yang-deficient, yin-deficient, phlegm-damp, damp-heat, blood-stasis, qi-stagnation, and special-diathesis (allergic). TCM holds that food has its own "nature," such as warming, cooling, or neutral, so the same food can help one constitution and aggravate another. The nine-type questionnaire is a standardized screening tool in China. Controlled evidence that matching food to constitution improves health outcomes is limited, so treat it as a framework for self-observation, not a diagnosis.

Worked example. Two roommates, Ana and Ben, both eat the same lunch: a bowl of raw cucumber and watermelon salad with iced green tea, about 350 kcal.

| | Ana | Ben | |---|---|---| | Typical pattern | Cold hands and feet, loose stools, prefers hot drinks | Feels hot, flushed face, oily skin, bitter taste in the mouth | | TCM type | Yang-deficient | Damp-heat | | Effect of the lunch | Feels bloated and chilled afterward | Feels refreshed | | TCM reasoning | Cooling foods burden a body already short of warmth | Cooling, fluid-clearing foods ease heat |

Same food, opposite responses. In TCM terms, Ana would be steered toward warm cooked dishes such as ginger-and-lamb soup or congee with cinnamon. Ben would be steered away from greasy, fried, or spicy food (such as hot pot) and toward mung beans, bitter melon, and barley.

Problem-solving application. Use a four-step routine:

  • Observe. For two weeks, log what you eat and how you feel 1–3 hours later (energy, digestion, temperature, sleep). A simple notebook works.
  • Group the patterns. Look for repeated links, such as "cold drinks, then cramping" or "fried food, then breakouts and heaviness."
  • Match to a type. Compare your pattern with the nine descriptions. Many people fit more than one, and TCM allows for a main type plus secondary tendencies.
  • Adjust one variable at a time. Change a single item, such as swapping iced drinks for warm ones, for a week and compare your log.

    • Practice problem. Mei often feels tired after meals, catches colds easily, and has a weak voice. Her diet is mostly raw salads and smoothies. Which type does this suggest, and what is one change to test?

    Answer. The pattern fits qi deficiency. TCM would suggest cooked, easily digested foods such as rice porridge, sweet potato, and chicken soup. She can test replacing one raw smoothie a day with warm porridge and track her energy.

    Caution. A constitution is not a medical diagnosis. Persistent fatigue, chronic diarrhea, or unexplained weight change needs a physician's evaluation. Food allergies must be managed by conventional medical rules, whatever your TCM type. Dietary changes should also keep total energy and nutrient intake adequate.
