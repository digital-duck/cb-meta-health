The Spleen–Stomach System (脾胃)

You've met the Five Phases as a way of grouping organs into families that support and check one another. The Spleen and Stomach belong to the Earth phase — think of Earth as the "soil" phase, since farmland is where raw materials become nourishment. In TCM, the Spleen–Stomach pair does something similar inside the body: they sit in the middle burner (中焦), the digestive zone roughly where your stomach and upper intestines are, and their job is to turn food and drink into usable material for the rest of the body.

Here's the division of labor TCM describes. The Stomach "receives and ripens" food — it's the first stop, breaking things down. The Spleen then "transforms and transports": it extracts the useful essence from digested food and drink and sends it upward and outward to the lungs and heart, where it becomes part of the qi and blood you learned about earlier. TCM calls this extracted material "food qi" (谷气). The Spleen is also said to "govern transformation of dampness" — when it's working well, fluids move properly; when it's weak, TCM describes symptoms like bloating, loose stools, or a heavy, foggy feeling as "dampness" accumulating.

Simple rule: Stomach breaks down, Spleen extracts and distributes. Together they're the root of postnatal qi — the energy your body makes daily from food, as opposed to the prenatal qi you inherited at birth.

These are traditional descriptive categories, not measured physiological processes, so don't expect lab tests for "Spleen qi."
