Stress and Digestion

You already know the feeling: a big test coming up, and your stomach feels tight or "off." That's not random — it's your nervous system making a real, physical trade-off.

Your body runs two competing modes. The fight-or-flight response prepares you for danger by sending blood to your muscles, heart, and lungs — the systems you'd need to run or fight. But digestion is a resource-heavy background process: it needs blood flow to the gut lining, steady muscular contractions (motility) to move food along, and a steady release of digestive enzymes and stomach acid. When fight-or-flight activates, the body treats digestion as expendable. Blood vessels to the gut constrict, motility slows, and secretion of digestive juices drops.

Here's the structural pattern worth remembering: the body allocates its resources based on priority, not availability. It doesn't ask "do I have enough blood for both?" — it asks "which system matters more right now?" Under perceived threat, digestion loses every time, even though there's no actual danger, like before an exam.

This matters practically: eating a big meal while anxious or rushed means your digestive system starts the job under-supplied — less blood, weaker contractions, fewer enzymes — so digestion is slower and less complete than the same meal eaten while calm.
