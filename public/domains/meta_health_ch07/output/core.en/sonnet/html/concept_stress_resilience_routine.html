A Daily Stress-Resilience Routine

You already know that a single deep breath can calm a racing heart before a test. That works because slow breathing shifts your nervous system from "fight-or-flight" toward "rest-and-digest," which lowers heart rate and lets your gut do its job. A stress-resilience routine takes this one idea and repeats it on purpose, at set points in the day, instead of waiting for stress to build up first.

In TCM terms, this routine protects digestion from what's called "Wood overacting on Earth" — when built-up tension (the Liver's Wood energy) pushes too hard on the digestive system (the Spleen's Earth energy), you may notice a tight stomach, poor appetite, or bloating after an argument or a stressful class. This is a traditional framework, not a scientifically measured mechanism, but the daily habits behind it are worth trying regardless of which explanation you use.

A simple four-part routine:

  • Before each meal: three slow breaths (roughly 4 seconds in, 6 seconds out) to shift digestion into a calmer state.
  • When tension builds: a "liver-releasing" movement — stretching, shaking out your arms, or a short walk — to physically discharge stress rather than let it sit.
  • In the evening: a calm wind-down (dim lights, no screens, gentle stretching) to prepare for sleep.
  • Feedback: track your heart rate variability (HRV) with a wearable, or simply journal one line about your stress level each night, so you can see whether the routine is actually working for you.

    • Practice problem: If your resting heart rate is 78 bpm before a stressful moment and drops to 66 bpm after three slow breaths, what percentage decrease is that? (Round to the nearest whole percent.)
