The HPA Axis

Think back to the stress response: your body noticing a threat and firing up to deal with it. That quick reaction is only the opening move. The hypothalamus, pituitary, and adrenal glands — together called the HPA axis — are the chain of command that keeps the response running once it starts.

Here's how the chain works. The hypothalamus, a small region at the base of the brain, senses stress and releases a signaling hormone. That hormone travels a short distance to the pituitary gland, which responds by releasing a second hormone into the bloodstream. That second hormone travels all the way down to the adrenal glands, sitting on top of each kidney, telling them to release cortisol — the main stress hormone. Cortisol then circulates through the body, raising blood glucose for quick energy, redirecting resources away from digestion, and sharpening alertness.

The pattern worth remembering: each gland in the chain releases a hormone that triggers the next gland, like a relay race with three runners. When cortisol levels get high enough, they also signal back to the hypothalamus and pituitary to slow down — a feedback loop that normally keeps the whole system in balance. Chronic, unrelenting stress can disrupt this feedback loop, and scientists actively study how that disruption affects long-term health, though many of the details are still being worked out.
