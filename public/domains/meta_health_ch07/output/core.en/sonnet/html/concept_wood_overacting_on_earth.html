Wood Overacting on Earth (肝木克脾土)

You already know that the Five Phases keep each other in check through a controlling cycle: Wood normally restrains Earth the way tree roots hold soil in place, keeping digestion steady. But TCM describes a situation where that restraint goes too far — "overacting" (相乘, xiangcheng) — and Wood doesn't just control Earth, it attacks it.

Here's the mechanism, as TCM explains it. When Liver qi becomes stuck — often from ongoing stress, frustration, or suppressed emotion — that stagnant qi presses on the Spleen and Stomach's job of transforming food into usable qi and blood. The result is a cluster of digestive complaints: bloating after meals, poor appetite, loose stools or bowel upset, especially when they flare up around stressful periods. This pattern is called 肝脾不调, "Liver-Spleen disharmony."

Notice this isn't the ordinary controlling relationship anymore — it's that relationship amplified beyond what the body can absorb, so Earth's function collapses instead of being gently guided. In modern terms, this description overlaps with something well-documented: the gut-brain axis, where psychological stress measurably alters gut motility and appetite through the nervous and hormonal systems. That physiological link is scientifically supported; the specific Liver-Spleen framework explaining why is a traditional model, not one confirmed by evidence.
