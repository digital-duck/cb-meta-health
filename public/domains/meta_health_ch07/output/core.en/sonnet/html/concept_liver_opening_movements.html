Liver-Opening Movements

By now you know that in TCM the liver is thought of as the organ most connected to the smooth flow of qi through the body — and that when this flow gets "stuck," people traditionally describe feelings of frustration, tension along the sides of the ribs, or a short temper. Two specific movements are said to work on exactly this: rib fanning (扇肋) and Baduanjin form 7, "clench fists with angry eyes" (攒拳怒目).

Rib fanning is a stretching-and-rotating motion that opens and closes the space between the ribs on each side of the torso, following the traditional pathway that the Liver channel is said to run along the sides of the body. The "fanning" image comes from how the arms sweep outward and the chest expands sideways, as if opening a fan against the ribs.

Baduanjin form 7 adds intensity: the practitioner makes fists, punches forward with a focused glare, and pulls back with gripping tension in the hands. In TCM theory, the eyes are considered the sensory opening connected to the Liver, and the deliberate "angry eyes" gaze combined with the clenching fists is said to direct and discharge pent-up qi rather than let it stagnate.

Simple pattern to remember: opening movements (rib fanning) stretch the channel pathway, while intensifying movements (form 7's glare and clench) engage the associated sense organ and add force.

Important note: no controlled scientific studies have confirmed that these movements measurably affect liver function or qi flow — their reported effects on mood or tension relief are traditional claims, not medically established ones.

Practice problem: A student practices rib fanning for 8 breaths, then Baduanjin form 7 for 6 repetitions, then repeats this pair 3 times. How many total breaths of rib fanning and total repetitions of form 7 has the student done?
