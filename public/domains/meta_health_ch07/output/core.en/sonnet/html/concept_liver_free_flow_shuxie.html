The Liver Governs Free Flow (肝主疏泄)

You've likely felt this before: stress builds up, and suddenly your stomach feels tight or you lose your appetite. In Traditional Chinese Medicine, this everyday experience points to one of the Liver's core jobs — keeping qi (气, the body's vital energy and activity) flowing smoothly, a function called "free flow" or shuxie (疏泄). Think of the Liver as a traffic controller for qi, making sure it moves through the body without getting stuck.

When free flow works well, three systems benefit. First, emotions stay balanced — smooth qi movement is linked to a calm, flexible mood, while blocked qi is traditionally associated with frustration, irritability, or a "stuck" feeling. Second, digestion runs smoothly, since the Liver's coordination helps the Spleen and Stomach process food efficiently; poor free flow is traditionally linked to bloating or an unsettled stomach. Third, the menstrual cycle depends on steady qi flow, since irregular movement is traditionally associated with irregular or uncomfortable cycles.

It's important to be clear: qi, and "free flow" itself, are traditional concepts, not measurable physical quantities like heart rate or blood glucose. There's no equation for how "smoothly" qi flows. Modern science hasn't established qi as a physical substance, though some of the associated patterns — like stress affecting digestion — overlap with things researchers do study, such as the gut-brain connection.

The general pattern TCM describes: smooth qi flow → stable mood, steady digestion, regular cycles. Blocked qi flow → emotional tension, digestive discomfort, cycle irregularity.
