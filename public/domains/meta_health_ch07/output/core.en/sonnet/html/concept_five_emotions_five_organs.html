The Five Emotions and the Five Organs

You already know that stress makes your heart race and worry can upset your stomach — your body and your feelings are connected. Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) built a detailed map of these connections thousands of years ago, pairing each of the five organs from the Five Phases system with one dominant emotion.

The traditional pairings are: anger harms the Liver, excess joy harms the Heart, overthinking harms the Spleen, grief harms the Lung, and fear harms the Kidney. Notice the word "excess" — TCM does not claim that feeling angry or joyful is bad. The idea is that when any one emotion is intense, prolonged, or unresolved, it is thought to disturb the qi (vital energy) and function of its paired organ over time. A person stuck in constant frustration, for example, is traditionally described as straining their Liver's ability to keep qi flowing smoothly.

This is a traditional framework, not a scientifically established mechanism — modern medicine does not show that specific emotions damage specific organs in this direct way. It is best understood as an early, systematic attempt to connect mental and physical health, and it still shapes how TCM practitioners ask questions about a patient's emotional life today.

Simple pattern to remember: Liver–anger, Heart–joy, Spleen–overthinking, Lung–grief, Kidney–fear — five organs, five emotions, one idea: feelings and body are linked, and balance matters more than the emotion itself.
