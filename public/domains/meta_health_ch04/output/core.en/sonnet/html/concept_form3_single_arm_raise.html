Form 3: Raising One Arm (调理脾胃须单举)

Recall Form 2's stance: feet grounded, breath slow, arms guiding movement rather than muscling through it. Form 3 adds a new shape — one arm presses upward toward the sky while the other presses downward toward the ground, opposite hands stretching in opposite directions at the same time.

Look at what this does to your torso. When one arm reaches up and the other reaches down, your rib cage lifts on one side and your waist lengthens on the other. This isn't a small stretch — it's a full diagonal opening through the trunk, right through the region where the stomach and upper intestines sit. In Traditional Chinese Medicine, this movement is framed as regulating two organs: the Spleen and the Stomach, which together are considered responsible for digestion, described traditionally as "raising the clear and lowering the turbid" — the idea that nourishing, useful substances should rise while waste should descend. This is a traditional framework, not a claim verified by modern physiology, but the physical logic is straightforward: alternating compression and stretch through the abdomen can encourage gentle mechanical movement in that area, similar to how twisting or bending affects digestion in ordinary exercise science.

The pattern to notice: opposite arms, opposite directions, sustained stretch, then switch sides. Each rotation should be slow enough that you feel the stretch through your entire side, not just your shoulder.
