Form 2: Drawing the Bow (左右开弓似射雕)

Picture the last time you took a huge breath before shouting across a field — your shoulders pulled back, your chest opened wide, and your arms probably swung out to help. Form 2 captures that exact motion on purpose: from a stable horse stance (feet wide, knees bent, weight sunk low), one arm draws back like pulling a bowstring while the other extends forward like aiming an arrow, then the sides switch. The name "shoot the eagle" comes from archers of the past drawing a bow to aim skyward.

What's actually happening in the body here is worth noticing. The horse stance gives your legs a fixed base, so all the movement and stretch happens through your torso and arms. As you draw the bow, your chest expands and your shoulder blades pull toward your spine — this is the same chest-opening action used in Form 1, but now driven by an active pulling motion instead of a passive raise. In TCM theory, this form is traditionally described as stretching the Lung and Large Intestine meridians, which run along the front of the chest and the inside/outside of the arm. Like other meridian claims, this is a traditional framework, not something confirmed by scientific studies on meridian pathways.

The pattern to remember: horse stance for stability, alternating draw-and-extend arms for the chest-opening stretch, controlled breathing paired with each pull.
