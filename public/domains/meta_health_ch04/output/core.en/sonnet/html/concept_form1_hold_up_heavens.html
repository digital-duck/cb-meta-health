Form 1: Holding Up the Heavens (两手托天理三焦)

You already know the basic move: interlace your fingers, press both palms overhead as if pushing up the sky, then release. What's actually happening when you do this?

Interlacing the fingers and pressing upward stretches the entire front and back of the torso at once — the diaphragm lifts, the ribcage expands, and the muscles along the spine lengthen from the pelvis to the base of the skull. This is why Baduanjin practitioners call it the "master key": every other form in the set works one region of the body, but Form 1 opens the whole trunk before any of that regional work begins.

In TCM theory, this stretch is said to open the pathway for the triple burner (三焦, sanjiao) — a traditional organizational concept dividing the torso into upper, middle, and lower zones, each associated with a cluster of organ functions. Traditional practice holds that if the trunk is compressed or tense, movement of qi between these three zones is restricted, so opening the whole torso first is believed to prepare the other seven forms to work more effectively. This idea of qi zones and their "opening" is a traditional model, not one with an anatomical structure confirmed by modern research — though the physical stretch itself does measurably increase rib cage mobility and diaphragm excursion, which is well established.

Simple rule: stretch the whole trunk first, work the parts second — sequencing, not just a single move.
