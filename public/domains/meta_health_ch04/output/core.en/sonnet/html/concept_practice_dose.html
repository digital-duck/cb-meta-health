How Much to Practice

If you've been trying the movements from this chapter, you're probably wondering how much is actually enough. The answer is refreshingly small: about 10 minutes per session — one or two rounds through the sequence — done once or twice a day.

Why so short? Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) practice is built around the idea of gentle, repeated stimulation rather than intense exertion. Think of it less like a workout and more like watering a plant a little each day. TCM holds that pushing too hard — to the point of heavy sweating or exhaustion — actually works against the goal, because it "consumes qi" (the vital energy the practice is meant to cultivate). This is a traditional claim, not one confirmed by controlled scientific studies, but it does align with a real physiological idea: prolonged heavy sweating causes measurable fluid and electrolyte loss, and overtraining without recovery can leave you more fatigued, not less.

Timing matters too, in TCM's framework. A morning round is said to help "raise yang" — the body's active, warming energy — helping you transition from rest into the day. An evening round is meant to "release tension," settling the nervous system before sleep. Neither yang nor qi can be measured with a stopwatch or a blood test, so treat these as traditional guidelines for structuring your day, not medical facts.

Simple rule: short and consistent beats long and occasional — 10 minutes, once or twice daily, stopping well before fatigue or heavy sweating.

Practice problem: If you practice one 10-minute round every morning and every evening for one full week, how many total minutes will you have practiced?
