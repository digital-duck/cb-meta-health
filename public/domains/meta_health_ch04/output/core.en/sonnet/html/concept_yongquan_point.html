The Yongquan Point (涌泉, KI-1)

You've already met Yongquan, the "Gushing Spring," as the starting point of the Kidney channel on the sole of the foot. Now let's look more closely at where it sits and what it's traditionally believed to do.

Yongquan is located on the bottom of the foot, roughly at the junction of the front third and back two-thirds of the sole — in the depression that appears when you curl your toes downward. It's the very first point on the Kidney meridian (KI-1), meaning that in TCM's model of energy flow, this is where Kidney qi is said to rise up from the ground and enter the body's channel system, before traveling upward through the leg, torso, and chest.

In TCM theory, Yongquan is considered a "grounding" point — a place where the body's descending, calming energy can be encouraged to settle downward, away from the head. Traditional practitioners have used stimulation of this point (through massage, acupressure, or acupuncture) for concerns like restlessness, insomnia, or a racing mind, based on the idea of drawing excess energy from the upper body back down. It's important to note that these effects are described within TCM's traditional framework and are not established by controlled clinical research in the way a drug's mechanism would be; some small studies suggest possible relaxation effects, but the evidence base remains limited.

Simple rule: KI-1 sits at the "spring" where the Kidney channel begins, on the sole's centerline, about one-third of the way from toes to heel.
