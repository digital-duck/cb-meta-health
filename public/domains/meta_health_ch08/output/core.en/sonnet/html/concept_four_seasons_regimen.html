Four Seasons Regimen

You already know the four-part rhythm: spring for growth, summer for expansion, autumn for gathering, winter for storing (春生夏长秋收冬藏). This isn't just poetry — it's a model that maps human physiology onto the yearly cycle of yang qi (阳气), the body's active, warming energy.

Think of yang qi like a wave with a predictable shape across the year: rising in spring, cresting in summer, receding in autumn, and pooling deep in winter. Each season asks something different of you because your internal state is tracking that wave, whether you notice it or not.

Spring (生, growth): yang qi is rising but still fragile. Rule: rise early, move gently, avoid crushing new plans with rigid discipline — let things unfold.

Summer (长, expansion): yang qi is at its peak, outward and active. Rule: stay active later into the evening, eat lighter, don't suppress sweating (it's the body venting excess).

Autumn (收, gathering): yang qi begins withdrawing inward. Rule: sleep earlier, calm the mind, start conserving rather than expending.

Winter (藏, storing): yang qi is stored deep, conserved for spring. Rule: sleep longest, avoid heavy exertion, keep warm, eat more nourishing/dense foods.

The pattern behind the rule: timing your activity level to match, not fight, the season's yang-qi phase. Living out of phase (e.g., staying up late and sweating hard in winter) is treated as one root cause of seasonal illness.
