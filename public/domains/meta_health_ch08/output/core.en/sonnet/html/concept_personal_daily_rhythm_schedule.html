Your Personal Rhythm Plan

You already know your body runs on a 24-hour clock: sleepy at night, hungry in the morning, more alert by midday. A personal rhythm plan takes that intuition and turns it into an actual daily schedule you can follow, built around real physiology plus traditional Chinese medicine's map of which organ is thought to be most active in each two-hour block.

Structurally, the day breaks into stages. Morning starts with light exposure — sunlight helps reset your circadian clock by suppressing melatonin — paired with gentle movement like Baduanjin (a set of eight slow qigong stretches) to wake up circulation and muscle tone. Breakfast lands in the traditional stomach-time window, roughly 7–9 a.m. (辰时), when digestion is considered strongest. Lunch, the day's largest meal, sits near midday when your body is most metabolically active, followed by light movement (even a 10-minute walk measurably lowers post-meal blood glucose spikes). Dinner comes early and light, since evening digestion is traditionally seen as weaker, and metabolic studies do show your body processes glucose less efficiently at night. An evening wind-down — dimming lights, slowing down — prepares you for sleep before 23:00, aligning with your natural melatonin rise.

The general rule: eat your biggest meal when your body is most active, and taper food and stimulation as the day winds down, sleeping before your body's natural melatonin surge fades. This plan also adjusts seasonally — winter schedules trend toward more sleep, summer toward earlier mornings — matching each season's traditionally associated organ and phase, though this specific seasonal-organ pairing is a traditional framework, not an evidence-based claim.
