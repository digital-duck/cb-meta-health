Caring for the Organ of Each Season

By now you know each of the five organs pairs with a season: Liver–spring, Heart–summer, Spleen–late summer, Lung–autumn, Kidney–winter. This idea, called seasonal organ care, goes one step further: it's a set of traditional living guidelines for supporting each organ when its season arrives.

The logic (in TCM theory) is that a season's qi is strongest during its own months, which gives that organ extra support — but also extra strain if you don't cooperate with it. So traditional practice recommends adjusting sleep, food, mood, and activity by season. In spring, keep the Liver's qi moving: wake earlier, stretch, eat lighter greens, and avoid bottling up frustration, since anger is the emotion traditionally linked to the Liver. In summer, protect the Heart: stay cool, keep a steady mood, and avoid overexertion in extreme heat. In late summer's humidity, support the Spleen with warm, easily digested food rather than raw or greasy meals. In autumn's dryness, care for the Lung with hydration and gentler breathing exercises. In winter, conserve energy for the Kidney: sleep more, keep warm, and avoid overexertion.

A simple pattern to remember: each season, favor habits that ease the paired organ's characteristic stress — movement and release in spring, calm and coolness in summer, warmth and simplicity in late summer, moisture and gentleness in autumn, rest and conservation in winter.

It's worth being clear: this seasonal framework comes from centuries of TCM observation, not from controlled scientific studies — some seasonal health patterns (like winter mood dips) are recognized by modern medicine, but the organ-specific mechanisms described here are not.
