Day Night Yin Yang

Yin-yang isn't just a symbol — it's a way of tracking how energy states rise and fall in a repeating cycle, and one of the clearest places to see this is the 24-hour day.

From midnight to noon, yang is climbing. Yang represents activity, warmth, and outward movement — think of the sun rising, your body temperature ticking up, alertness building. From noon to midnight, yin takes over: growth turns to rest, outward energy turns to storage, and the body shifts toward recovery. This isn't two separate forces switching on and off — it's one continuous curve, like a wave that peaks and dips.

You can sketch this as a simple periodic pattern. If $t$ is the hour (0 to 24), yang strength roughly follows a rise-then-fall shape peaking near noon ($t = 12$), while yin follows the mirror-image pattern, peaking near midnight ($t = 0$ or $t = 24$). At any moment, yang and yin are complementary: when one is high, the other is low, and they cross around sunrise and sunset — the transition points.

Simple rule: Match your activity to the curve. Yang-dominant hours (morning to afternoon) favor exertion — exercise, focused work, digestion of a large meal. Yin-dominant hours (evening to night) favor slowing down — winding down, sleeping, letting the body store and repair.
