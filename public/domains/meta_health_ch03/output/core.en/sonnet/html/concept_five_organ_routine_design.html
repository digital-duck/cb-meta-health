Designing Your Five-Organ Routine

You've now practiced each of the five organ movements on its own. The next step is putting them together into a routine you actually do — a real sequence, timed to your morning or evening, matched to your body.

Start with sequencing logic. A common traditional approach follows the generating cycle order — Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water — moving Liver, Heart, Spleen, Lung, Kidney movements in that order, since practitioners describe each organ as "feeding" the next. If your goal is waking up energized, front-load the Liver and Heart movements, which use more expansive, faster breath. If your goal is winding down before sleep, reverse the order, finishing on the slower, grounding Kidney movement instead of starting there.

Pairing breath with movement is not optional — it's the mechanism connecting the physical form to its intended effect. Each expansion phase pairs with an inhale, each fold or press with an exhale, typically 4–6 seconds each, giving you a real, measurable respiratory rate of roughly 6–8 breaths per minute during the routine — slower than a typical resting rate of 12–16 breaths per minute. This slower rate is one physiological piece consistently linked to feelings of calm.

Scale each movement to your own range — a stiff shoulder or a bad knee should shrink the movement's arc, not the breath count. Finish every routine with the sealing finish: hands resting over the lower abdomen, a few quiet breaths, letting the body settle before you move on.
