Heart–Kidney Interaction (心肾相交)

You already know the Five Phases picture: Heart sits in the Fire phase, Kidney in the Water phase, and in the generating cycle Water actually feeds Fire, while in the everyday physiology of the body the two organs are said to check and support each other constantly. This pairing has its own name in traditional Chinese medicine (TCM): Heart–Kidney interaction, sometimes described as "Heart Fire descends and Kidney Water ascends."

The idea works like a loop. Heart Fire, associated with warmth, alertness, and mental activity, is said to descend toward the Kidneys, keeping Kidney Water from becoming too cold and stagnant. In turn, Kidney Water is said to ascend to the Heart, cooling Heart Fire so it doesn't flare into restlessness, anxiety, or insomnia. When this exchange is smooth, TCM describes the person as calm, clear-headed, and sleeping well. When it's disrupted, practitioners use the term "Heart–Kidney disharmony" to describe a pattern often linked to symptoms like insomnia, night sweats, or a racing, anxious mind — though it's important to say plainly that this specific Fire-Water exchange model is a traditional framework, not something confirmed by modern physiological measurement.

The general pattern to remember: Heart and Kidney are described as regulating each other in a continuous two-way exchange, not each one acting alone. This is a relationship to picture and describe in words — not an equation.
