Dantian (丹田)

You've heard that qigong practice centers on the breath sinking to a point below the navel. That point is called the lower dantian (丹田), and now it's worth understanding it more precisely: what it is, why it matters, and how it fits into a broader traditional map of the body.

In Traditional Chinese Medicine and qigong practice, the lower dantian is located roughly three finger-widths below the navel, inside the abdomen — not on the skin, but in the body's interior. Traditional theory describes it as a reservoir where essence, qi, and spirit are gathered, refined, and stored. It's considered the root of steady breathing, physical stamina, and calm focus. This is a traditional anatomical-energetic concept, not a location that shows up on an MRI or in physiology textbooks — modern science hasn't identified a distinct organ or structure there.

What practitioners can observe, though, is something training-related: deep diaphragmatic breathing naturally pulls the diaphragm downward and expands the lower abdomen outward, which is physically consistent with "settling the breath" near this point. Directing attention there during practice is a concentration technique — a way of anchoring focus and slowing respiration, similar to how modern meditation apps cue you to "breathe into your belly."

A simple guiding rule from qigong: inhale, let the lower abdomen expand gently outward (as if filling toward the dantian); exhale, let it draw back in. This is sometimes called abdominal or dantian breathing.

Practice problem: Sit comfortably and place one hand on your belly, three finger-widths below your navel. Take 5 slow breaths, trying to make your hand rise on the inhale and fall on the exhale. Write one sentence describing how your breathing rate or calmness changed compared to your normal breathing.
