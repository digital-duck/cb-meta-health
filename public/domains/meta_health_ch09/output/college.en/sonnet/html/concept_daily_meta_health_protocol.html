The Daily Meta-Health Protocol (元健康)

Definition. The daily Meta-Health protocol (日常元健康方案) is a 24-hour routine that treats movement, eating, mind and rhythm as one practice rather than four separate habits. Here 元 means "the whole above the parts"; it does not mean 代谢 (metabolic health). The protocol is a starting template. You observe your own responses and adjust it over weeks, so it is not a fixed prescription.

The 24-hour blueprint.

| Time | Practice | Purpose | |---|---|---| | Morning | Daylight exposure, then Baduanjin (八段锦), about 10–15 minutes | Light anchors the body clock. Baduanjin is gentle, low-intensity exercise. | | Before each meal | 3–5 slow breaths | Shifts attention and slows the start of eating | | Meals | Eat slowly, stop at 70–80% full | Leaves room for fullness signals to arrive | | 30–40 min after eating | Easy walking or light movement | Gentle activity after meals can blunt the post-meal glucose rise | | Through the day | Short stress-release breaks (breathing, stretching, a brief walk) | Prevents stress from accumulating | | Evening | Early, light dinner | Leaves time to digest before bed | | Before 子时 (23:00) | Be asleep | Protects sleep length and regularity |

In TCM, going to sleep before 子时 is linked to nourishing the body at night. The practical reason is simpler: an earlier bedtime makes a full 7–9 hours easier when the morning alarm is fixed.

Worked example. Suppose Mei wakes at 6:30 and works until 18:00. Her draft schedule:

  • 6:30 light by the window, then 12 minutes of Baduanjin
  • 7:30 breakfast
  • 12:15 lunch, with three breaths first and a stop at about 75% full
  • 12:50 a 15-minute walk, which falls 35 minutes after she starts eating
  • 15:30 five minutes of stretching and slow breathing
  • 18:30 a light dinner
  • 22:15 lights out, so she is asleep well before 23:00

    • Problem-solving application: personalize and adjust. The protocol only works if you test it against your own data. Choose one or two simple measures and track them for two weeks. Examples are bedtime, wake time, afternoon energy (rated 1–5) and, if you have a glucose monitor or a clinician's guidance, post-meal readings.

    Say Mei finds her afternoon energy is still 2 out of 5 on three of five workdays. She asks what differs on those days. Her log shows that on those days lunch ran late and the walk was skipped. She changes one variable by protecting a 12:15 lunch and a short walk. After another week she compares the ratings.

    Follow three rules when adjusting:

  • Change one thing at a time, so you know what caused any improvement.
  • Keep the practices you can sustain. A realistic 70% adherence beats a perfect plan abandoned in a week.
  • Adapt to your situation. Shift work, pregnancy, diabetes, or medication use call for advice from a clinician, especially for meal timing and exercise.

    • The protocol is lifestyle support. It does not replace medical care for any diagnosed condition.
