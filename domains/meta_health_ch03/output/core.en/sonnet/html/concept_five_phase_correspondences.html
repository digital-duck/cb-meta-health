Five-Phase Correspondences

You already know the Five Phases—Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water—as a cycle of relationships between the organs. TCM theory extends this same five-part pattern outward, mapping each phase not just to an organ but to a whole cluster of things: a season, an emotion, a flavor, and a body tissue. This is the Five-Phase correspondence table.

| Phase | Organ | Season | Emotion | Flavor | Tissue | |---|---|---|---|---|---| | Wood | Liver | Spring | Anger | Sour | Tendons | | Fire | Heart | Summer | Joy | Bitter | Blood vessels | | Earth | Spleen | Late summer | Worry | Sweet | Muscles | | Metal | Lung | Autumn | Grief | Pungent | Skin | | Water | Kidney | Winter | Fear | Salty | Bones |

The logic isn't causal in the way a biology pathway is—it's associative. Traditional practitioners used it as a diagnostic map: if a patient showed excessive anger alongside tendon stiffness, both symptoms pointed toward the Liver system, even though anger and tendons aren't connected by any known physiological mechanism. The table gave clinicians a way to organize observations that seemed, at the time, to cluster together.

It's important to be direct here: this correspondence system is a traditional framework, not a scientifically validated one. Modern research hasn't found real physiological links between, say, "Grief" and "Lung," or "Sour" and "Liver." Treat the table as a historical and philosophical lens for understanding how TCM organizes the body—useful for interpreting old texts and practices, not as clinical fact.
