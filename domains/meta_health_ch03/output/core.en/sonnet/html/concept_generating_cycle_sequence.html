The Generating-Cycle Sequence (相生顺序)

You already know the Five Phases feed one another in a set order: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water, and back to Wood. This generating cycle isn't just a diagram — it's also a real sequence of movements you can practice in your own body, one phase warming up the next.

The routine works like this: rib fanning (Wood) opens the sides of the torso and gets circulation moving. That movement flows into a deep squat (Fire), which builds heat and works the legs and core. From there you rotate into a torso twist (Earth), which uses the momentum and warmth already generated to mobilize the spine. That twisting motion transitions into chest expansion (Metal), opening the upper body and lungs. Finally, body lifting (Water) — rising onto the toes or extending upward — draws on the stability built through the previous four movements.

The pattern here is sequence and support, not cause-and-effect in the biomechanical sense: each movement is traditionally understood to prepare the body for the one that follows, similar to how a good warm-up routine builds from small movements to bigger ones. This ordering claim comes from TCM theory, not from exercise science research — there's no published evidence that doing movements in this specific order produces better results than doing them in another order, though the individual movements (squats, twists, stretches) are well-established for mobility and circulation.
