The Triple Burner (Sanjiao, 三焦)

You've already met qi flowing through the body, guided by the Five Phases and carried in the meridians. Now zoom out and picture the torso as a building with three floors, each handling a different stage of transforming food and air into usable energy. TCM calls these three floors the Triple Burner: upper, middle, and lower.

The upper burner covers the chest — heart and lungs — and is described as a "mist," spreading qi and fluids outward like fine spray, the way breath carries oxygen through the whole body. The middle burner covers the stomach and spleen, described as a "foam" or churning vat, where food is broken down and turned into raw material for qi and blood. The lower burner covers the kidneys, bladder, and intestines, described as a "drainage ditch," sorting out what the body keeps from what it releases as waste.

The simple pattern to remember: upper burner disperses, middle burner transforms, lower burner separates and excretes. Traditionally, these three "burners" are also considered a network connecting the other organs, coordinating how fluids move through the body as a whole — but note that the Triple Burner does not correspond to any single organ found in anatomy dissection; it's a traditional functional model, not an established anatomical structure, and modern science has not identified a matching physical organ.
