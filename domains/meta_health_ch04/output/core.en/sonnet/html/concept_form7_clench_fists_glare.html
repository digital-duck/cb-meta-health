Form 7: Clenched Fists, Glaring Eyes (攒拳怒目增气力)

Recall Form 6 (rib fanning), where a slow twist gently released tension gathered in the Liver's pathway. Form 7 flips the mood: instead of a soft release, you build power on purpose, then let it out through a punch and a stare.

Stand in a horse stance — feet wide, knees bent, spine upright, like sitting on an invisible stool. From here, make a fist at your hip, then punch forward slowly, rotating the fist so your palm turns from up to down as your arm extends. As you punch, widen your eyes and fix your gaze forward, as if glaring at an opponent. Then draw the fist back and repeat on the other side.

In traditional Chinese medicine, the Liver is said to "open into the eyes," meaning the eyes are considered a window through which the Liver's condition shows itself and can be influenced. Glaring is traditionally described as a way to direct and vent Liver qi — a term for the body's vital circulating function — that has become stagnant or excessive, similar to how rib fanning released it through gentle stretching. Clenching the fist tightly is thought to build strength and resolve, which traditional practice pairs with the glare to sharpen focus and intensity. These are traditional explanations, not claims verified by modern physiology, though the horse stance does place real, measurable demand on the quadriceps and core muscles, and controlled breathing during exertion is a well-documented way to help regulate heart rate.

Practice problem: If you hold the horse stance for 30 seconds per punch and do 6 punches per side (12 total), how many total seconds do you spend in the stance? Show your multiplication.
