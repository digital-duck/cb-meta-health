Prenatal and Postnatal Qi (先天与后天)

You already know that the Spleen and Stomach make qi and blood every day out of food — that's postnatal qi (后天之气), the "after-birth" supply your body rebuilds constantly. But TCM describes a second source: prenatal qi (先天之气), the qi you're born with, inherited from your parents and stored in the Kidneys. Together these two sources explain why your body has both a lasting foundation and a daily income.

Think of prenatal qi as capital and postnatal qi as income. Capital is fixed at birth — you don't get more of it, and TCM holds that it slowly depletes over a lifetime, which is one traditional explanation for aging. Income, by contrast, is renewable: eat well, digest well, and the Spleen and Stomach replenish qi and blood daily, regardless of how much capital remains.

The two aren't independent, either. Traditional theory holds that abundant postnatal qi can nourish and support the Kidneys' prenatal reserve, while a person born with weaker prenatal qi can still function well if postnatal qi production stays strong — a case for consistent nutrition and healthy digestion. Note that "prenatal qi" and "aging as depletion of stored qi" are traditional TCM concepts, not something measured or established by modern physiology; they're a framework for thinking about vitality and constitution, not medical facts about genetics or metabolism.
