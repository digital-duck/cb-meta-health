Liver Qi Stagnation (肝气郁结)

You already know the Liver's job in TCM is to keep qi moving smoothly through the body, the way a good manager keeps a project running without bottlenecks. Liver qi stagnation is what happens when that flow gets blocked — usually by frustration, stress, or emotions that get swallowed instead of expressed.

Think of qi movement like traffic on a road system. The Liver channel runs along the sides of the body, through the flanks and up into the throat and chest. When emotional "cars" pile up instead of moving through, pressure builds at predictable points along that route. That's why the classic signs cluster exactly where the Liver channel travels: tightness or distention in the rib area (flank pain), a constant urge to sigh (the body's attempt to force a release), irritability or mood swings, and a strange sensation of something stuck in the throat that won't swallow or come up — sometimes called "plum-pit qi" (梅核气).

The pattern practitioners look for is simple: symptoms that are movable, come and go with mood, and worsen under stress, paired with a wiry pulse quality. This is a traditional diagnostic framework, not a measured physiological finding — modern science doesn't verify "qi" as a substance, though the link between chronic stress and physical tension (tight shoulders, breath-holding, throat tightness) is well documented.
