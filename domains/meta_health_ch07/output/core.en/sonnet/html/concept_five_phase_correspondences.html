Five-Phase Correspondences

You already know the Five Phases as a cycle — Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water feeding and checking one another. Now let's look at the full correspondence table TCM builds around each phase, because this is where the framework becomes genuinely useful for describing a person, not just an abstract cycle.

Each phase is traditionally linked to a body organ system, a season, a color, a taste, and — importantly for this chapter — an emotion:

| Phase | Organ (Zang) | Season | Emotion | |-------|-------------|--------|---------| | Wood | Liver | Spring | Anger | | Fire | Heart | Summer | Joy | | Earth | Spleen | Late Summer | Worry/Pensiveness | | Metal | Lung | Autumn | Grief | | Water | Kidney | Winter | Fear |

The pattern to notice: this is a classification system, not a causal equation. TCM tradition holds that each organ has an emotional tendency it's especially sensitive to — for example, the Liver is said to be disturbed by prolonged anger, and the Kidney by chronic fear. This is a traditional framework passed down through centuries of clinical observation, not a scientifically validated biological mechanism, and modern research has not confirmed that specific organs "store" specific emotions. Still, the broader idea that mood and physical state influence each other is well-supported by science — chronic stress does measurably affect the body.

The rule to hold onto: correspondences describe patterns of association, not cause-and-effect chains, and belong in words and tables, never equations.
