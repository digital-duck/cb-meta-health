Payoff

By now you have six separate pieces: when to let light hit your eyes, when to move your body, when to eat your first and largest meals, how to time gentle movement after eating, when to wind down, and why sleep should start before 23:00 (子时, Zishi). A rhythm plan is what happens when you stop treating these as six separate rules and stitch them into one ordinary day. That is why it is the natural endpoint of this book: everything else was a single instrument, and this is the piece of music.

A basic weekday version might look like this: wake with morning light and a few minutes of Baduanjin, eat breakfast during Chenshi (辰时, roughly 7–9 a.m.) when digestive qi is traditionally described as strongest, take your largest meal near midday, walk gently for 10–15 minutes afterward, eat a lighter dinner earlier in the evening, dim lights and slow down an hour before bed, and be asleep before 23:00 so the night's metabolic repair processes can run uninterrupted. Across the year, the plan shifts: in seasons whose associated organ (in the Five Phase framework) is under more seasonal demand, movement and food choices adjust to support it — more warming, denser food and slower mornings in winter; lighter food and earlier rising in summer. This seasonal layer is traditional guidance, not a scientifically established prescription — but the underlying pieces (light exposure timing, meal timing, exercise timing, sleep timing) each rest on real physiological evidence.
